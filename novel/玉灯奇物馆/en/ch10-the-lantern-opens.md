# Chapter 10 · The Lantern Opens

## I. The People Came Under the Lamp

On the night of the reopening, Jade Lantern Curio was full for the first time. Not a sale — a small "True Curios Class" — and the faces were all familiar: Claire, Daniel, Mrs. Okafor, Theo, Priya, Elena, and Noah leaning in the doorway, the old book in his hand somehow already set quietly on the counter.

Mei had no notes. She only laid a few curios on the low table: a box of incense, a string of obsidian, a vermilion bracelet. Then she asked everyone to say which one had kept them company through a night.

Claire said that after the night of the clear quartz, she'd slept a full six hours for the first time. Daniel ran his thumb over the lotus bracelet and said his mother's photo now sat on the nightstand, no longer hidden in a drawer. Theo raised both rings and said which job he took no longer mattered — what mattered was finally daring to tell his parents, *I'm afraid.* Priya laughed that the roll-down door now stayed open every day, coffee scent reaching the corner. Elena held up the returned fake and said she'd later bought a real vermilion bracelet — "not for magic, but to remember that someone took me seriously enough to talk."

In the lantern's glow, every face loosened. Noah nudged his book toward the middle of the counter, as if to say: this place is worth it.

## II. I Understand

After they left, Mei went back to the incense corner and lit the jade lantern. On the ring finger of her left hand, her grandmother's **Jade Growth Ring** (p04) gave a soft green answer in the light — the one she'd first put on in the basement of Chapter 4 and never taken off since. She opened *The Curio Record* to the page where the dried calming herb was pressed, and said to the lamp:

"I understand you now, grandmother."

A draft came through the window seam; the flame swayed, as if answering.

She closed the book, her fingertips tracing the worn edge of the cover. She had once feared taking the shop, feared being tied down by the word *mystery*, feared she wasn't worthy. Only now did she see — her grandmother had never guarded a mystery. She had guarded *care*: listening to a person seriously, getting a culture right, lighting one lantern for the right person.

## III. One Was Missing

The next day, Mei went down to the basement to inventory the collection against her grandmother's list. The cedar chest still bore its four-season labels; the little notes on each box were still clear: *for those who can't sleep, for those who've gone, for those who finally speak.*

She checked box by box, and at the end stopped before an empty velvet case. The case was empty; the padding held a rounded impression — once it had cradled a small jade cup that kept the lantern company.

She turned to the last page of the notebook. Her grandmother's hand was heavier there, as if written when strength was nearly spent:

*If the lamp stands and the cup is gone, do not panic. Follow the light to find it.*

Below, a lighter line, as if added later:

*It was taken, not lost. Whoever took it knew the light.*

Mei held the empty case in her palms. Where the cup had gone, who had taken it, why they had known the light — she knew none of it. But her grandmother's words fell like a seed into soil: the lamp does not go out, the thread is not yet cut; things will wander, but the story remains.

She stood, set the empty case gently back in its place, blew out the lamp, and went upstairs.

Outside the door, the obsidian bead hung in the night, quiet as an unfinished promise.

And she already knew where the next stretch of the road would lead — following the light, to find the cup that had been taken.

*(End of Volume One)*
