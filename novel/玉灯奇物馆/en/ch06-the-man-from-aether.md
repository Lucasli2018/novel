# Chapter 6 · The Man from Aether

## I. A Suit Too Bright

The bell over the door rang sharper than usual that afternoon.

The man who stepped in wore a suit too bright, a matte-black tie, and cufflinks that threw back the light. He introduced himself — Julian Vance, founder of Aether & Co. — leaning in just slightly, the way people do on television when they want you to trust them.

"Miss Lin, your shop… has a certain flavor," he said, glancing around, letting his eyes rest a moment on the jade lantern. "I'm in the Eastern-spirituality business. I've been hoping to partner with a little store like your grandmother's."

Mei was wiping the counter, still wearing the jade ring her grandmother had left her. She didn't offer him a seat. She just folded the cloth and set it aside.

Vance slid a gilt booklet across the wood. Its cover read *The Aether Eastern Energy Collection*. Inside, every page was full of words — *blessed*, *energy matrix*, *healing frequency*. The crystals in the photos glowed pink and purple, like candy.

"We'd love to bring old shops with a story — like your grandmother's — into the fold," he said smoothly. "Make it a chain. Pop-ups. Social. Help young people fall for the East — our way, of course."

When Mei heard the words *Eastern spirituality*, the thin calluses on her fingertips tightened a little. She thought of a line in her grandmother's notebook: *objects connect people to memory; they are not product symbols.* And here was a man trying to pull the symbol out of the memory and wire it to a battery.

She didn't cut him off. She turned, took a stick of **Sandalwood & Tonka Bean Incense** (p48) from the shelf, lit it, and set it in the burner. The smoke rose slowly, and the room filled with a warm, unhurried sweetness — like a well-loved armchair. The scent was honest, a different world from the cold *energy matrix* on the page. She let the fragrance hold her, and didn't let his pace carry her off.

"This incense of yours," Vance said, wrinkling his nose. "A little old-fashioned, isn't it?"

"The old-fashioned things," Mei said quietly, "are the ones that survive a second sniff."

## II. Your Grandmother Said the Same

Vance talked for a good ten minutes before the turn came.

"Funny thing," he said. "Your grandmother turned me down once, you know."

Mei's hand slipped on the incense tongs.

She remembered the page in the basement notebook — a certain capital-backed brand had wanted to swallow the shop and turn it into fast-selling "spiritual goods," and her grandmother had refused, drawing a small lantern in the margin with the note *the lamp stays, the person stays*. That "capital-backed brand" had been Aether's earlier name.

She looked up and studied the man for the first time. He told it lightly, like an old acquaintance, even a little wistful: "The old lady was stubborn. Missed her best chance. Now she's gone, and you should be practical — hand the shop to us, take the money, keep the story. Everyone wins."

Mei set the tongs back by the burner. She did not smile.

"My grandmother chose her things well. They don't fake," she said, and closed the gilt booklet, sliding it back toward him. "This shop isn't for sale."

Vance raised an eyebrow, smile calibrated to a decimal. "The trend is on my side, Miss Lin. Young people today want pretty, photogenic, *feel-good*. That little lantern of yours won't last long."

He left a business card and let himself out. The door closed, the bell swayed, and the jade lantern kept burning.

When he said *won't last long*, Mei opened the drawer and took out a **Yellow Jade Stability Bracelet** (p26), slipping it back onto her wrist. The honey-yellow jade was warm — the color of *the season when you're holding everything together*. She closed her hand around it and let the word *steady* settle into her palm. Four words, for herself: *don't rush*.

## III. Confirmation

After he left, Mei pulled open the drawer and took out three pinned notes.

The first: Daniel had mentioned a "very trendy crystal shop" where the "love stone" he bought did nothing — *the lights were nice, but no one listened*.

The second: Mrs. Okafor's neighbor had grumbled about a "chain spirituality store" that talked her into expensive stones which did nothing at all.

The third: the basement page where her grandmother recorded the refused takeover, naming the brand that later changed its name and kept doing "Eastern" business.

She lined the three notes up in a row, like a single thread running from the corner shop to her own counter. They weren't three stores. They were one — Aether.

Mei weighed the notes down with an obsidian bead, and suddenly the little lantern her grandmother had drawn felt heavy with meaning. She opened the notebook and, beside *the lamp stays, the person stays*, wrote four careful words:

**This time, it's me.**

Outside the window, the empty store across the street had sprouted a ring of pink neon scaffolding. On it, the familiar words: *blessed*, *energy*, *healing*.

Aether's pop-up was opening right across the street.
